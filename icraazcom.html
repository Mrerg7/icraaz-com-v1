INFECTION CONTROL RISK ASSESSMENT

What every contractor and facility leader in Arizona needs to know.

ICRA is not optional. It's a mandatory, multidisciplinary process that protects patients during construction and renovation in occupied healthcare facilities.

The ICRA Team

Multidisciplinary group led by infection preventionists. Contractors must engage early to provide critical input on workflows, barriers, and equipment that clinicians may not visualize from blueprints alone.

Planning & Design

Number and placement of isolation rooms, air handling in ORs and labs, water systems to control Legionella, patient flow, and strategic contaminant control measures.

During Construction

Class I–IV precautions based on risk level. Negative air pressure monitoring with alarms, daily checklists, vibration management around sensitive procedures, and coordinated utility shutdowns.

Risk Mitigation

Patient relocation planning, temporary HVAC and water system modifications, demolition protection, emergency suspension procedures, and clear delineation of responsibilities between all parties.

Specialized Training

Contractors and workers need targeted training (ASHE, union programs, etc.). Hospitals and GCs increasingly require proof of ICRA competency before awarding work.

Joint Commission & CMS Compliance: Violations during construction can jeopardize Medicare/Medicaid funding and accreditation. Proactive ICRA collaboration between hospitals and contractors is non-negotiable.
